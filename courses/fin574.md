## M1 Scarcity Allocation Markets
* Economics is defined as the science that studies the allocation of scarce goods and services.
* Scarcity is a fundamental concept, applying even to resources like clean air.
* The concept of opportunity cost highlights that every choice involves giving up the value of the next best alternative.
* A demand curve is a representation showing the quantities consumers wish to purchase at different prices.
* Demand curves are typically downward sloping, illustrating the inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.

## M2 Govt Intervention
* A price ceiling is a government-imposed limit setting the maximum price for a good or service.
* Rent control is presented as a common real-world example of a price ceiling.
* Elasticity measures how responsive the quantity demanded is to changes in price.
* Demand can be classified as inelastic, elastic, or unit elastic based on the magnitude of the elasticity value.
* For a linear demand curve, demand is elastic in the upper portion and inelastic in the lower portion.

## M3 Firms Production Costs
* The core behavioral assumption in the theory of the firm is that firms aim to maximize profits.
* Economic profit is calculated as total revenue minus total cost, crucially including opportunity costs.
* The term "marginal" in economics signifies the change in a variable resulting from a one-unit change in another.
* The Law of Diminishing Marginal Product states that adding successive units of an input will eventually lead to smaller increases in output.
* A firm's marginal cost curve is typically U-shaped and intersects the average total cost curve at its lowest point.

## M4 Firm Behavior
* Firms maximize profits by choosing the output level where marginal revenue equals marginal cost.
* For a firm operating in a competitive market, marginal revenue is equal to the market price.
* Profits can be calculated graphically as the area corresponding to quantity times the per-unit profit margin (price minus average total cost).
* Firms earn positive economic profits in the short run if the market price is greater than their average total cost.
* The MR=MC rule identifies the profit-maximizing output on the upward-sloping portion of the marginal cost curve.

## M5 Perfect Competition
* Market structures are categorized along a spectrum from monopoly (one firm) to perfect competition (many firms).
* Perfect competition is characterized by a large number of small players, homogeneous products, free entry/exit, and perfect information.
* The market supply curve in a competitive industry is the horizontal summation of the individual firms' supply curves.
* An individual firm's supply curve corresponds to its marginal cost curve above the minimum average variable cost.
* Under ideal conditions, the market outcome in perfect competition is considered socially optimal, maximizing total welfare.

## M6 Monopoly Markets and Efficiency
* Consumer surplus represents the value consumers receive from a product above the price they paid.
* The demand curve reflects consumers' marginal willingness to pay at different quantities.
* The benevolent dictator is a conceptual tool used to evaluate market outcomes based on their overall efficiency or total welfare.
* A monopoly is defined as a single supplier in an industry with no threat of new firms entering.
* A monopolist maximizes profit by producing where marginal revenue equals marginal cost and sets the price based on the demand curve at that quantity.

## M7 Oligopoly and Game Theory
* Oligopoly describes a market structure with a small number of firms, where the actions of one firm significantly impact others.
* Unlike competitive or monopolistic markets, outcomes in oligopoly are often strategic and interdependent, not purely deterministic.
* Firms in an oligopoly may have an incentive to collude to restrict output and increase prices, acting like a cartel.
* A key challenge for colluding firms is the inherent individual incentive to cheat on the agreement.
* Game theory is a framework used to analyze strategic interactions and predict outcomes, such as the Nash Equilibrium.

## M8 Market Failures
* Market failure occurs when a market, left to itself, results in an inefficient allocation of resources from a societal perspective.
* The Winner's Curse is a phenomenon in auctions where the winning bidder often overestimates the value of the item and pays more than its worth.
* Externalities are a type of market failure where the cost or benefit of an activity impacts a third party not directly involved in the transaction.
* Negative externalities lead the market to produce more of a good than is socially optimal because the full social cost is not borne by the producer or consumer.
* Positive externalities lead the market to produce less of a good than is socially optimal because the full social benefit is not captured by the producer or consumer.
