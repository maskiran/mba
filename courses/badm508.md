#### M1 Leadership Defined
*   Leadership is a socially constructed process and relationship, context-dependent, requiring accountability.
*   It involves empowering others and avoiding command-and-control approaches.
*   Historical perspectives include Trait, Behaviorist, Contingency, Transactional, Transformational, and Relational approaches.
*   Key leadership models discussed include Charismatic, Servant, and Authentic leadership.
*   Leaders play different roles like coach, mentor, and sponsor and should focus on being learners.

#### M2 Strategic Self Leadership
*   Self-leadership is understanding and intentionally guiding oneself towards desired experiences, involving self-control and self-determination.
*   Core competencies include self-awareness (internal/external), planning, decision making, and optimizing motivation.
*   Effective decisions involve identifying alternatives, navigating biases, using tools like Descartes Square, and knowing yourself.
*   Ethical decision making considers normative/behavioral ethics, values, and barriers like "TRAGEDIES".
*   Psychological well-being includes hedonic, eudaimonic, and resilience aspects, with PERMA outlining key components.

#### M3 Leading Others
*   Leading others focuses on building trusting relationships and collaborating across various organizational boundaries.
*   Emotional Intelligence (perceiving, using, understanding, managing emotions) is presented as a core skill for leaders.
*   Trust building relies on the ABI model (Ability, Benevolence, Integrity) and is influenced by neurobiology (oxytocin/cortisol).
*   Effective leaders understand and ethically use sources of power (Legitimate, Reward, Coercive, Expert, Referent, Informational).
*   Navigating differences and conflict effectively involves understanding conflict types and approaches (e.g., TKI framework: Competing, Collaborating, etc.).

#### M4 Growing As Leader
*   A Leadership Growth Mindset is built upon three pillars: Curiosity, Courage, and Commitment.
*   Curiosity drives learning, innovation, and creativity through asking questions and exploring assumptions.
*   Courage involves acknowledging and embracing vulnerability (uncertainty, risk) and shifting from blaming to internalizing.
*   Commitment requires overcoming excuses and setting clear, actionable goals, potentially using the SMART framework.
*   Discipline, essential for sustained growth, involves regular practice, connecting with others, knowing purpose, and a systematic approach (Design, Develop, Evaluate, Adjust).

#### M5 Team Culture Defined
*   Teams are groups working toward a goal, with dynamic work that requires a focus on interpersonal processes.
*   Effective teaming requires interpersonal awareness, inquiry, and the ability to teach others.
*   Organization culture is defined as a pattern of assumptions developed by a group over time, taught to new members.
*   Schein's Iceberg model depicts culture in layers: visible artifacts/behaviors, espoused values, and deep underlying assumptions.
*   Principles of effective teamwork are performing well, benefiting the team members, and improving capacity over time.

#### M6 Cultures Of Safety
*   Cultures of Safety are built on psychological safety – a team climate where people feel comfortable being their authentic selves without fear.
*   Key practices for building safety include setting the stage, showing vulnerability, focusing on outcomes, appreciation, routines, and active inclusion.
*   Safe cultures encourage problems and mistakes to surface for learning, applying principles like Hanlon's Razor (assuming best intentions).
*   Leaders foster safety by admitting concerns, being the last to share opinions, and addressing systemic issues like those related to hierarchy or ethics that threaten safety.
*   Safety promotes benefits such as better teamwork, innovation, learning, risk reduction, and a strong sense of belonging.

#### M7 Culture Of Engagement
*   Cultures of Engagement involve committed employees driven by a sense of purpose in their work.
*   Motivation comes from Extrinsic (rewards) and, more powerfully, Intrinsic (Autonomy, Competency, Relatedness) sources; leaders should focus on intrinsic drivers.
*   Leaders boost engagement through building trust, transparency, establishing clear expectations, communicating vision, and providing necessary resources.
*   Meaningful work, relating tasks to personal and broader significance, is vital for engagement, satisfaction, and health.
*   Individuals can actively cultivate meaning through job crafting (altering tasks, relationships, or cognitive perspectives).

#### M8 Cultures Of Growth
*   Cultures of Growth encourage continuous learning, prioritizing the gaining and sharing of knowledge individually and organizationally.
*   They require psychological safety to admit mistakes and enable learning from experience and failure.
*   Growth involves shifting from being "knowers" to "learners" and empowering problem-solving using structured reflection tools like the PDCA cycle.
*   These cultures foster creativity by generating ideas that are both novel and useful through perspective shifts.
*   Navigating constant change requires understanding complex systems, addressing resistance to routines, and building trust relationships.
