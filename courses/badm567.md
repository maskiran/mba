## M1 Operations Strategy
* Operations strategy involves both strategy formulation and implementation steps.
* Order qualifiers are the minimum standards customers expect from a product or service.
* Order winners are the criteria that differentiate a product or service and lead customers to choose it.
* For fast food, low price and quick delivery can be order qualifiers.
* For luxurious restaurants, attentive service and personalization can be order winners.

## M2 Process Config And Analysis
* A job shop configuration offers high variety and flexibility but is high in cost and not suited for high volume.
* Workers in a job shop typically require a broader set of skills compared to assembly line workers.
* Different products in a job shop can follow unique sequences through departments.
* Process mapping involves identifying and sequencing activities in a process.
* When mapping a process, you should decide the level of detail and plan for measuring relevant metrics.

## M3 Inventory Management Concepts
* Inventory consists of stocks of items held at various points to meet demand.
* Inventories act as a buffer between demand uncertainty and supply disruptions.
* Inventory helps protect against unexpected changes in market demand and supply disruptions.
* Holding inventory can allow businesses to leverage price advantages like bulk discounts or anticipated price increases.
* Inventories can be classified based on the stages of the production process, such as finished goods.

## M4 Inventory Management Applications
* Continuous review and periodic review are two different policies for managing inventory stock.
* The reorder point for an item typically includes expected demand during lead time and a buffer stock.
* Safety stock is a component of the reorder point calculated using demand variability and desired service level.
* Under a periodic review policy, the average daily inventory and expected costs can be computed using specific formulas.
* The optimal order quantity in a one-shot inventory model aims to maximize expected profit under uncertain demand.

## M5 Quality
* Service quality can be assessed using five dimensions: Reliability, Assurance, Tangibles, Empathy, and Responsiveness (RATER).
* The Customer Gap model identifies differences between customer expectations and perceptions at various stages of service delivery.
* The Total Cost of Quality framework categorizes costs into those for good quality (Prevention, Appraisal) and poor quality (Internal/External Failure).
* The eight dimensions of product quality include Performance, Features, Conformance, Reliability, Durability, Serviceability, Aesthetics, and Perceived Quality.
* The DMAIC framework (Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control) structures improvement projects.
* Process improvement initiatives like Lean and Six Sigma aim for significant reductions in defects and waste.

## M6 Process Control
* Statistical Process Control (SPC) charts use control limits (UCL, CL, LCL) derived from process data to monitor variation over time.
* Points falling within the SPC chart limits indicate common cause variation and an in-control process.
* Points falling outside the SPC chart limits indicate special cause variation, requiring investigation into root causes.
* Variable control charts (like X-Bar and R charts) are used in pairs for continuous measurement data to track average and variation.
* Common root cause analysis techniques include Affinity diagrams, Pareto analysis, Fishbone diagrams, and 5-Why analysis.

## M7 Supply Chain Management
* Supply Chain Management (SCM) integrates suppliers, manufacturers, warehouses, and stores to efficiently produce and distribute merchandise while minimizing system-wide costs and meeting service levels.
* Key pillars of Supply Chain Strategy include competencies, processes, resource allocation, and basis of product competitiveness.
* Supply chain contracts specify the extent of coordination, sharing of risk and rewards, and participation in joint improvement efforts.
* Types of supply chain contracts include Wholesale price, Buyback, and Revenue-sharing contracts.
* The Bullwhip Effect describes the phenomenon where variability in orders increases as one moves upstream in the supply chain.

## M8 Operation Resilience
* Designing and sustaining improvements is a key area of focus for operations.
* Reverse logistics involves processes for managing used products, including remanufacturing and recycling.
* Supply chain network design involves determining the optimal flow of goods between suppliers, warehouses, and retailers.
* Important technologies for improving supply chain efficiency include RFID, IoT, Artificial Intelligence, and Blockchains.
* RFID systems can be used for tracing and tracking inventory and assets in supply chains.
