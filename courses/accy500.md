## M1 Introduction to Financial Accounting
* Financial accounting provides information to external users.
* The accounting equation: Assets = Liabilities + Equity.
* Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) guide financial reporting.
* The four basic financial statements are income statement, balance sheet, statement of cash flows, and statement of changes in equity.
* The accounting cycle includes recording transactions, preparing adjusting entries, and closing the books.

## M2 Recording Transactions
* Double-entry accounting requires equal debits and credits.
* The chart of accounts organizes accounts by category.
* Journal entries record transactions chronologically.
* The general ledger contains all accounts and their balances.
* Trial balances verify that debits equal credits.

## M3 Adjusting Entries
* Accrual accounting recognizes revenues and expenses when earned or incurred.
* Adjusting entries ensure proper matching of revenues and expenses.
* Types of adjusting entries include accruals and deferrals.
* Prepaid expenses and unearned revenues are deferrals.
* Accrued revenues and expenses are accruals.

## M4 Financial Statements
* The income statement shows revenues, expenses, and net income.
* The balance sheet presents assets, liabilities, and equity.
* The statement of cash flows shows cash inflows and outflows.
* The statement of changes in equity explains changes in equity accounts.
* Financial statements are interrelated and must be consistent.

## M5 Merchandising Operations
* Merchandising companies buy and sell inventory.
* Perpetual inventory systems track inventory continuously.
* Periodic inventory systems count inventory periodically.
* Cost of goods sold is calculated using beginning inventory, purchases, and ending inventory.
* Gross profit is sales revenue minus cost of goods sold.

## M6 Inventory Valuation
* Inventory cost includes purchase price and necessary costs to get inventory ready for sale.
* FIFO, LIFO, and weighted-average are common inventory costing methods.
* Lower of cost or market rule requires inventory to be written down if market value is lower.
* Inventory errors affect both the balance sheet and income statement.
* Inventory turnover ratio measures how quickly inventory is sold.

## M7 Internal Control
* Internal control helps achieve reliable financial reporting and compliance.
* The control environment sets the tone for the organization.
* Risk assessment identifies and analyzes relevant risks.
* Control activities are policies and procedures to mitigate risks.
* Monitoring activities assess the quality of internal control over time.

## M8 Cash and Receivables
* Cash includes currency, bank deposits, and cash equivalents.
* Bank reconciliations compare bank and book balances.
* Accounts receivable arise from credit sales.
* Allowance for doubtful accounts estimates uncollectible accounts.
* Notes receivable are formal promises to pay with interest.
