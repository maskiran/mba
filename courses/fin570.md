## M1 Introduction to Corporate Finance
* Corporate finance focuses on maximizing shareholder value.
* The three main areas are capital budgeting, capital structure, and working capital management.
* Financial statements provide essential information for decision-making.
* Time value of money is fundamental to financial analysis.
* Risk and return tradeoff affects financial decisions.

## M2 Financial Statement Analysis
* Ratio analysis helps evaluate company performance.
* Liquidity ratios measure ability to meet short-term obligations.
* Profitability ratios assess earnings performance.
* Leverage ratios evaluate debt levels.
* Market ratios relate stock price to financial metrics.

## M3 Time Value of Money
* Present value discounts future cash flows.
* Future value compounds present amounts.
* Annuities are series of equal payments.
* Perpetuities are infinite series of payments.
* Interest rates affect present and future values.

## M4 Capital Budgeting
* Net Present Value (NPV) is the primary decision criterion.
* Internal Rate of Return (IRR) is the discount rate that makes NPV zero.
* Payback period measures time to recover investment.
* Profitability Index relates present value to initial investment.
* Capital rationing occurs when funds are limited.

## M5 Risk and Return
* Expected return is the weighted average of possible returns.
* Risk is measured by standard deviation or variance.
* Beta measures systematic risk relative to the market.
* The Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM) relates risk to expected return.
* Cost of capital is the required return on investments.

## M6 Capital Structure
* Capital structure is the mix of debt and equity.
* Modigliani-Miller propositions form the basis of capital structure theory.
* Financial leverage affects risk and return.
* Optimal capital structure minimizes cost of capital.
* Bankruptcy costs affect capital structure decisions.

## M7 Dividend Policy
* Dividend policy affects shareholder returns.
* Factors affecting dividend policy include earnings, growth opportunities, and tax considerations.
* Stock repurchases are an alternative to dividends.
* Dividend irrelevance theory suggests dividends don't affect value.
* Signaling theory suggests dividends convey information.

## M8 Working Capital Management
* Working capital is current assets minus current liabilities.
* Cash management optimizes cash balances.
* Inventory management balances costs and service levels.
* Accounts receivable management affects cash flow.
* Short-term financing includes bank loans and trade credit.
