## M1 Money and Finance
* Money serves as a means of payment, unit of account, and store of value.
* Payment methods have evolved from commodity money to modern electronic systems like digital wallets and cards.
* Money is a specific type of asset used for payments and is distinct from overall wealth.
* The financial system facilitates the flow of funds from savers to borrowers via markets and institutions.
* Historically, money growth showed a positive link with inflation, though this correlation is less evident in recent low-inflation environments.

## M2 Modern Banking
* Commercial banks raise funds primarily through deposits and grant various types of loans.
* The lending process involves loan origination, financing, and servicing, which can be separated in the originate and distribute model.
* Investment banks engage in activities such as securities underwriting and trading, generating significant revenues.
* Bank balance sheets illustrate how assets like loans and securities are funded by liabilities and equity capital.
* Significant off-balance sheet activities include managing undrawn loan commitments and derivatives positions.

## M3 Risk and Return
* Bank financial performance can be assessed using measures like return on assets (ROA) and return on equity (ROE), the latter often amplified by leverage.
* Leverage boosts returns in good times but increases vulnerability to losses in bad times.
* Key risks for banks include credit risk (defaults), interest rate risk, liquidity risk (meeting obligations), market risk (trading losses), and operational risk (failures).
* Credit risk is managed by evaluating borrowers, pricing loans based on risk, and using contracts with covenants.
* Liquidity risk arises from potential cash outflows and the need to quickly convert assets, sometimes at a loss.

## M4 Regulation
* Central banks act as a lender of last resort to provide liquidity to banks during times of stress.
* Financial institutions are overseen through regulation and supervision, including periodic examinations.
* Stress tests are a regulatory tool to ensure large banks can withstand adverse economic conditions by holding sufficient capital.
* The U.S. banking structure includes a few dominant megabanks alongside a larger number of smaller community banks.
* Shadow banking refers to financial activities conducted by institutions that perform bank-like functions but are outside traditional banking regulation.

## M5 Interest Rates
* Monetary policy primarily targets short-term interest rates in key funding markets like the federal funds and repo markets.
* Government securities, particularly U.S. Treasuries, are crucial instruments for implementing monetary policy due to their liquidity and risk-free nature.
* The yield curve, which shows interest rates across different maturities, is a closely watched indicator of market expectations about the economy.
* Money market mutual funds are major short-term lenders in the financial system and serve as substitutes for bank deposits.
* Securitization bundles loans into tradable securities, which can increase credit supply and are relevant to monetary policy transmission.

## M6 Federal Reserve
* The Federal Reserve operates under a Dual Mandate to achieve maximum employment and stable prices.
* The Federal Reserve performs five key functions, including monetary policy and promoting financial stability.
* Traditional monetary policy relies on tools like Open Market Operations and the discount window to influence bank reserves and interest rates.
* When short-term interest rates are near zero, the Federal Reserve employs unconventional tools such as forward guidance and large-scale asset purchases (quantitative easing).
* The Federal Reserve's balance sheet is dominated by government securities held as assets and the monetary base as liabilities.

## M7 Monetary Policy and Real Economy
* Monetary policy actions are intended to influence macroeconomic outcomes, specifically economic growth, unemployment, and inflation.
* Central banks face a recognized trade-off between controlling inflation and reducing unemployment.
* Changes in the target interest rate transmit through financial markets, affecting borrowing costs and ultimately household and business spending decisions.
* Rules like the Taylor Rule offer a framework for setting interest rates based on macroeconomic conditions, reflecting the central bank's objectives.
* Empirical analysis indicates that changes in monetary policy can lead to predictable responses in economic variables like output, employment, and prices.

## M8 Financial Crises
* Financial crises highlight the central bank's critical role in providing liquidity to maintain stability.
* Historical events like the Great Depression illustrate how bank failures and subsequent declines in money supply can severely impact the real economy.
* Common causes of financial crises include over-reliance on volatile short-term funding and high levels of leverage.
* The 2008 financial crisis demonstrated how stress in non-bank financial sectors, such as markets for asset-backed commercial paper, can disrupt credit flows.
* Responses to financial crises often involve central bank lending facilities and coordinated fiscal measures like government spending and transfers.
