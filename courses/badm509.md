#### M1 Purpose And Governance
*   Business purpose is debated between prioritizing shareholders (rational view) and broader stakeholders like employees, customers, and communities (natural/open view).
*   From a stakeholder perspective, profits are a means to address diverse needs and create long-term value, not the sole *goal*.
*   Integrating purpose, especially making a difference for others, increases employee motivation, job meaning, and pro-social behavior.
*   Mission statements, used by most large firms, articulate 'what' and 'why', needing top leadership commitment, specific function alignment (strategy/purpose), and careful construction ('what, why, how, who') for impact.
*   Organizational governance can be seen through Agency Theory (rational/control) or a natural system lens (human element), with effectiveness measured multifacetedly.

#### M2 Org Structure
*   Organizational structure, vital for all managers, involves dividing labor, coordinating tasks, and allocating decision authority (centralized/decentralized, tall/flat hierarchy).
*   Contingency Theory states there's no single best structure; effectiveness depends on the specific context.
*   Common structures include Functional (grouping by activity, emphasizing expertise/efficiency but internal focus/hard coordination) and Divisional (grouping by product, geography, or customer, enhancing adaptability/external focus but risking duplication/lack of coordination).
*   The Matrix structure uses a dual hierarchy (e.g., functional and project/divisional) to integrate competing pressures, requiring "two-boss managers".
*   Other structures include Network (outsourcing non-core tasks for flexibility) and Team-based (flexible, adaptable grouping for projects), though hierarchy remains fundamental for coordination.

#### M3 Growth
*   Organizational control mechanisms include Bureaucratic (rules, hierarchy, standardization - rational view), Clan (shared values - natural view), and Market (internal competition).
*   Organizational designs range from Mechanistic (control, efficiency, standardization, centralized authority, tall hierarchy) to Organic (more flexible, adaptable, decentralized authority, horizontal communication).
*   A Mechanistic design is characterized by high standardization, specialized jobs, clear hierarchy, vertical communication, and centralized decision-making.
*   Most real organizations are hybrids, combining mechanistic and organic elements in different parts based on function or need.
*   Beyond the fundamental hierarchy, organizations use various integrating mechanisms like direct contact, committees, task forces, and cross-functional teams to improve horizontal coordination, especially in larger or more adaptive firms.

#### M4 External Environment
*   Resource Dependence Theory highlights that organizations rely on the external environment for vital resources, leading to power dynamics based on dependence.
*   Firms manage resource dependencies and seek power by reducing their own reliance and increasing others' reliance on them, often through linkages like acquisitions or joint ventures.
*   Organizations face a challenge balancing Exploitation (refining existing capabilities for certain, short-term gains) and Exploration (experimentation for uncertain, long-term payoffs), often biased towards exploitation.
*   Adapting and staying successful long-term is hard due to rapid external change and difficulty predicting the future, evidenced by high turnover in lists like the Fortune 500.
*   Incumbents focusing on improving existing products for high-end customers creates opportunities for Disruptive Innovation, where new entrants offer simpler, cheaper alternatives that improve over time and challenge established players.

#### M5 Change
*   Organizational change is difficult, often failing, and requires understanding and using power – the ability to influence and get things done.
*   Power sources include positional (Legitimate, Reward, Coercive) and personal (Expert, Referent), plus control over resources (Resource Dependence Theory).
*   Personal networks are key to power; weak ties are particularly valuable for accessing new information and opportunities beyond one's immediate group.
*   Managers have tools to overcome resistance and build commitment: education/communication (often underestimated), participation, support, negotiation, co-optation, and coercion.
*   Major change implementation models, like Kotter's 8 steps, emphasize creating urgency, a guiding coalition, a compelling vision, communicating it, empowering action, short-term wins, consolidating gains, and institutionalizing change, noting the risk of underestimating effort.

#### M6 Managing Culture
*   Organizational culture exists on three levels: visible Artifacts (symbols, stories, rituals), espoused Values (stated beliefs, guiding principles), and deep Basic Assumptions (unconscious core beliefs).
*   Artifacts are observable expressions of culture, including dress, office layout, language, stories, and even formal systems like rules and incentives.
*   Values are what the organization states as important, influencing behavior and decisions, but may differ from actual practice ("cheap talk").
*   A strong culture has high consensus (shared values) and intensity (passionately held values).
*   Culture is shaped by leaders' actions, strategic decisions, hiring, and symbolic actions.

#### M7 Decision And Ethics
*   Decision-making is fundamental to organizational design and management, shaping strategy through the pattern of choices made.
*   Decision-making can be viewed through Rational (objective, optimization), Natural (human limits, biases, social factors), and Open (external influences) lenses.
*   Beyond the ideal Rational Model, real-world decisions are often Boundedly Rational, where managers satisfice (choose acceptable options) due to limited information/time; Intuition is faster but prone to error.
*   Human decision-making is prone to biases, including cognitive (e.g., Anchoring, Confirmation) and social (e.g., Groupthink, Similar to Me bias).
*   Ethical considerations are crucial but subject to Ethical Fading; breakdowns can arise from Ill-conceived goals (incentivizing unethical behavior, like at Wells Fargo) or rationalizations like Advantageous Comparison.

#### M8 Leadership In Orgs
*   Leadership can be understood using the three lenses: Rational (positional authority), Natural (relationships, human element), and Open (adapting to environment).
*   The Path-Goal Model is a contingency theory where leaders adapt their style (directive, supportive, participative, achievement-oriented) to clarify the path and support followers based on task and follower characteristics.
*   Transactional leadership involves exchanges (rewards/punishments), while Transformational leadership inspires followers towards a shared vision through key behaviors (Idealized Influence, Inspirational Motivation, Intellectual Stimulation, Individualized Consideration); combining both is often most effective.
*   Effective leaders are "clock builders" who create enduring organizational capabilities, not just "time tellers" whose skills are indispensable but don't build capacity.
*   Level 5 Leaders combine extreme professional will and personal humility, demonstrating this humility via the Window and Mirror Principle (crediting others for success, taking blame for failure).
