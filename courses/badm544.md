## M1 Leading Strategically
* Strategic leadership involves setting a company's mission, vision, and values and developing its business model.
* Mission, vision, and values provide purpose, coordinate action, resolve disputes, and inspire stakeholders.
* The VARS framework is a practical tool for analyzing business models.
* VARS includes: Value Proposition, Activities/Resources/Capabilities, Realization of Value (revenue), and Scope.
* SWOT analysis is a subjective starting point for strategic analysis, identifying opportunities and threats in the external environment.

## M2 External Analysis
* Analyzing the external environment is critical for strategic fit.
* The PESTEL framework is a comprehensive checklist for analyzing the macro-environment.
* The Five Forces framework is a powerful tool for analyzing industry profitability.
* The weakest of the Five Forces is the pivotal force determining overall industry profitability.
* Industry structural attributes like product differentiation significantly affect rivalry and profitability, as seen in the Soft Drink Industry.

## M3 Internal Analysis
* Internal analysis helps understand why companies perform differently and achieve competitive advantage.
* Competitive advantage stems from a firm's internal attributes (activities, resources, capabilities) that are valuable and rare.
* Sources of advantage can be analyzed through Activities (like value chains/networks), Resources (assets), and Capabilities (things the firm does well).
* Sustaining competitive advantage requires isolating mechanisms (barriers to imitation) and ensuring durability and relevance.
* The VRI framework (Valuable, Rare, Inimitable) summarizes the conditions for sustained competitive advantage.

## M4 Strategic Renewal
* Strategic positioning involves choosing generic business strategies like cost leadership or differentiation.
* Generic strategies can be pursued with either a broad or narrow market scope.
* While being "stuck in the middle" is risky, some firms can successfully pursue dual strategies.
* Strategic renewal may be necessary for businesses facing adversity and change.
* Effective strategic leaders should focus on a few critical actions, embrace change, and communicate a clear, inspiring vision.

## M5 Corporate Scope
* Corporate Scope includes decisions about Vertical Scope and Horizontal Scope.
* A key concept is that all organizational forms are flawed, requiring comparison of alternatives like markets vs. integration.
* Analyzing corporate scope often boils down to comparing make-or-buy alternatives.
* Value-creating diversification can be evaluated using the "better-off test" asking if combining businesses creates value.
* Comparing relative advantages and disadvantages of different organizational forms is crucial.

## M6 Corporate Transactions
* Major corporate transactions include Mergers and Acquisitions (M&A), Divestitures, and Strategic Alliances.
* The "Winner's Curse" is a potential problem in M&A where the winning bidder may overpay.
* Private equity firms often enhance value in acquisitions by improving management.
* Successful post-merger integration depends on focus and aligning activities with a clear value creation thesis.
* The "elements of value" frameworks provide a systematic checklist for building value propositions based on customer needs.

## M7 Global Strategy
* Global Strategy involves understanding globalization, foreign market entry strategies, and multinational enterprises.
* The Diamond Model helps analyze how a company's location contributes to its competitive advantage.
* Strategic choices available to multinational enterprises (MNEs) are explored.
* Globalization and its drivers are introductory topics.
* Expertise on global strategy is incorporated in the lectures.

## M8 Corporate Governance
* Topics include Stakeholders and Corporate Governance for public firms.
* Companies operate within a nexus of stakeholders, including internal and external groups.
* "Shareholder primacy" is a perspective emphasizing maximizing returns for shareholders.
* Key characteristics of a public firm include limited liability and separation of ownership and control.
* Corporate governance mechanisms aim to direct and control the firm and minimize agency costs.
