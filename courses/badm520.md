## M1 Marketing Process
* Marketing involves understanding consumer needs and using insights and messaging to entice them to choose a product.
* Needs are universal and drive behavior, while wants are individual preferences for satisfying needs, influenced by culture, social factors, resources, and behavior.
* Consumers buy products for the benefits they provide (core product), not just the physical item.
* The marketing mix (Product, Place, Promotion, Price) is a set of tools where the first three elements create value and Price captures it.
* Effective research involves identifying consumer needs, employing appropriate methods like primary research, and designing surveys carefully with simple language, avoiding ambiguity and leading questions.

## M2 Segmentation Targeting Positioning
* A value proposition clearly states the specific benefits, unique advantages, and problem solved for the ideal customer.
* Segmentation groups customers with different needs into manageable subgroups based on observable attributes.
* Effective segments must be measurable, substantial, accessible, differentiable, actionable, stable, and connected to data.
* Targeting involves selecting the segments a firm can best serve, aligning with business goals and focusing on the benefits important to those customers.
* Positioning establishes a brand's unique place in the market relative to competitors, often summarized internally in a positioning statement that includes the Unique Selling Proposition (USP).

## M3 The Power of Branding
* Brand equity is the added value associated with a brand name, driven by customer perceptions and associations.
* Strong brands build loyalty, reduce business risk, and increase overall brand value by consistently delivering on their brand promise.
* Leading global brands, such as Apple and Amazon, demonstrate high levels of brand equity.
* Successful brand extensions depend on having an appropriate fit with the original brand, providing leverage through existing associations, and addressing a market opportunity.
* A brand mantra is a short phrase capturing the core benefits of a brand, distinct from the more detailed positioning statement.

## M4 Influencing Consumer Behavior Marketing Plan
* Consumer decisions are influenced by reference groups, both associative (groups one belongs to) and aspirational (groups one wants to belong to).
* Prospect theory highlights that consumers are often influenced by perceived losses more than equivalent gains, leading to loss aversion and the endowment effect when considering new products.
* The way information is presented, or framing, can significantly impact consumer choices, even if the underlying options are objectively the same.
* Consumers use reference points and heuristics (shortcuts) to make pricing judgments, often relying on the prices of known signpost items or the appearance of prices ending in nine to infer value.
* A marketing plan requires both strategy (identifying opportunities, needs, segments) and tactics (the marketing mix); the initial situation analysis, including the 5 C's, is crucial for understanding the environment.

## M5 Creating Value - Product
* The marketing mix, consisting of Product, Place, Price, and Promotion, must function in harmony to effectively deliver value to target customers.
* The Product element of the mix is essentially the physical form and features through which value is packaged and delivered to the consumer.
* Product, Place, and Promotion are the elements of the marketing mix primarily responsible for creating value by delivering perceived benefits.
* Understanding the Five Product Levels, from the Core Product (fundamental benefit) to the Augmented Product (differentiation points), helps clarify how an offering provides value.
* The augmented product level is where firms typically compete and differentiate their offerings beyond the basic expected attributes.

## M6 Delivering Value - Place
* The distribution channel encompasses all steps and intermediaries required to move a product from the manufacturer to the final consumer.
* Channel design involves deciding the length (number of levels) and breadth (number of intermediaries) of the channel.
* Channel management is often overseen by the channel steward, the member possessing the most power within the channel structure.
* Disintermediation is the process of removing intermediaries to allow consumers direct access to products or services, often resulting in benefits for the consumer.
* Different channel models offer distinct competitive advantages, as seen in the comparison between the mobility and niche focus of food trucks versus the operational capacity of restaurants.

## M7 Capturing Value - Price
* Price serves as the mechanism to divide the value created between the customer (as an incentive to buy) and the firm (to cover costs and generate profit).
* Two main approaches to pricing are cost-plus pricing, based on production costs, and value-based pricing, based on the customer's perceived value of the benefits offered.
* True Economic Value (TEV) is the theoretical value a fully informed buyer would assign to a product, calculated from the cost of the next best alternative plus the value of the product's unique benefits.
* Consumer psychology heavily influences pricing perception through reference prices (internal and external) and the use of mental shortcuts (heuristics) like focusing on signpost items to gauge overall price levels.
* Price skimming is an initial pricing strategy for new products to maximize early revenue at the highest possible price, though it is not a sustainable long-term approach due to competitive entry and market saturation.

## M8 Communicating Value - Promotion
* Marketing communication, or Promotion, involves various tools used to inform and persuade consumers, including advertising, public relations, personal selling, sales promotion, and direct marketing.
* The effectiveness of advertising can be evaluated using frameworks like ADPLAN, which assesses aspects such as attention, distinction, clear positioning, and linkage to the brand.
* Public relations relies on earning credibility through third-party endorsements and requires consistent messaging across multiple online and traditional media sources.
* The 6 M's model provides a structured approach to planning communication campaigns, starting with strategic decisions about the Market, Mission, and Message.
* Different promotional elements are best suited for specific purposes, such as personal selling for high-value products or sales promotions like coupons and samples for driving immediate purchase.
